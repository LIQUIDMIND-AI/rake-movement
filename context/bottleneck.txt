The Core Paperwork BottlenecksRather than simple clerical typos, "incorrect paperwork" in the context of Indian steel imports usually refers to failure to satisfy specific, complex regulatory portals:SIMS 2.0 Registration Discrepancies: Under the Steel Import Monitoring System (SIMS) managed by the Ministry of Steel, importers must file detailed data on grades, origins, and end-uses. If registration is not submitted at least seven days prior to arrival, or if the HS Code classification on the Bill of Entry has an error, customs will freeze the shipment.The Missing BIS NOC: The single largest culprit for massive port delays is the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) Quality Control Order (QCO). If an imported steel grade does not match an existing Indian Standard, the importer must obtain a No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the Ministry of Steel.Suspended Clarification Committees: Delays often spike when administrative backlog occurs. For instance, massive backlogs accrued when the Quality Control Committee meetings responsible for processing these NOCs were temporarily suspended, causing an immediate pile-up of international shipments (notably from Japan and Vietnam) awaiting manual clearance.2. Scale of Impact on the IndustryThe Global Trade Research Initiative (GTRI) and local trade groups have highlighted the scale of this paperwork-and-policy deadlock:10,000+ Affected Units: Over 10,000 MSMEs and domestic steel-consuming units (automotive, aerospace, and manufacturing) face major supply disruptions due to container blockages.International Friction: The Embassy of Japan and Vietnam's Trade Ministry have repeatedly lodged formal complaints with India’s Ministry of Commerce regarding specialized raw steel consignments sitting at ports for nearly two months without clearance due to delayed administrative paperwork processing.3. Financial and Logistics PenaltiesWhen a steel shipment is flagged at Indian ports (like Nhava Sheva or Chennai) for incorrect or unlinked documentation, it enters a "Customs Query Raised" status. Until resolved, the cargo accumulates steep compounding fees:Demurrage Charges: Paid to the port authority for utilizing space.Container Detention Charges: Paid to the shipping line for holding the physical container.How to Check or Resolve a Specific ShipmentIf you have a specific steel consignment stuck at an Indian port, you can track its exact status using official customs portals:Check ICEGATE: Log into the Indian Customs Electronic Gateway (ICEGATE) to see if your Bill of Entry (BoE) has been flagged by the Risk Management System (RMS) or if a formal documentation query has been raised.Verify AD Code & IEC: Ensure that your Authorised Dealer (AD) Code is successfully registered for that specific port and your Importer Exporter Code (IEC) is active.Cross-Reference SIMS: Ensure the invoice value, weight, and HSN codes perfectly match what was registered on the DGFT SIMS portal.If you are currently managing a delayed shipment, share the specific port location or the exact error/query raised by customs.
